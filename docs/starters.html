Starters and depth — what is officially verifiable

The shortest honest answer: the NFL does not publish a pregame depth chart, so a "1st / 2nd / 3rd / 4th string" rank cannot be verified from an official source. What is official is the NFL gamebook, which lists the complete starting lineup by slot, the substitutions, the Did Not Play list and the Not Active list. That gives post-game starter identity, not a pregame depth order.

Why the string rank is blank

  • Roster page: the official team roster lists name, number, position, status, height, weight, experience and college. There is no depth-order column. Verified on the Bills roster page.
  • Depth charts on other sites: third-party charts exist, but they are not official NFL publications, so they cannot be presented here as official facts (Flag F18).
  • Gamebook: official, but it tells you who played, after the game. Using it as a pregame expectation (the previous week's lineup) is a modelling choice, not a published depth chart. See suggestion SR-04.

Offense and defense, separately

The requirement asks about defenses losing starters to second, third or fourth string, and about offenses playing starters or backups. Both sides are counted in the unit table above: Side separates Offense, Defense and Special teams, and the unit column separates QB, RB, WR/TE, OL, DL, LB, DB and ST. What cannot yet be said is who the starter was for each of those units in each game, because only one week of gamebooks has been verified.
