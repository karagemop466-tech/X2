The dated corpus tranche: v1 frozen, v2 published beside it

Why this page exists. The pre-registered backtest corpus is data/games_2026_results.csv — 137 games (weeks 1–9 = the brief's 136-game log, plus the Week 10 Thursday game). Thirteen Week 10 games played on 15 and 16 November 2026 are settled and were transcribed from the official by-week page, but editing a pre-registered corpus in place would silently change every published metric. So the 13 games are published as a parallel corpus (data/corpus_v2_2026.csv, 150 games) and v1 is left byte-frozen. This is proposal P09 / readiness D29 and D64 finally carrying a number instead of a request.

Does adding them change anything we publish?

The backtest cannot change, and that is checked rather than asserted. Every walk-forward evaluation week trains only on games strictly before that week, and all 13 added games are in Week 10, so for every evaluation week the training set is identical in v1 and v2 — 7 of 7 evaluation weeks, compared row set by row set in scripts/corpus_v2_effect.py. The 89-game out-of-sample record is therefore untouched by the tranche, and no historical metric is recomputed on this page.

Flagged: the seal and the published line disagree on one game (F110)

This was found while writing the check above, and it is an irregularity, not a rounding difference. The pre-kickoff forecast log is sealed with SHA-256 so a later edit is detectable. Recomputing that digest from the v1 fit reproduces the published seal (5aaf29db…) only when the neutral-site flag is ignored; applying the flag — which is what the published Week 11 line does — produces a different digest. The one fixture affected is the Mexico City game, MIN at SF: the sealed log froze 2.5 / 38.5 and the published line reads 2.9 / 38.9. All twelve other fixtures agree to the decimal, because no other Week 11 game is neutral. The cause is visible in scripts/model.py: the log is sealed with predict(main, home, away) while the same game is originated into model_lines_week11.csv with the neutral flag applied.

Nothing was overwritten. The published line is the correct treatment under F71 (a neutral site gets no home-field term), so reverting it would introduce an error; what is stale is the seal. Two ways to close, both owner decisions: (a) re-seal from the neutral-aware prediction and record the old digest as superseded, or (b) keep the v1 seal as the historical record and add a v2 seal beside it. Until one is chosen, the leaderboard stays unranked. Reproduce with python3 scripts/corpus_v2_effect.py; register data/forecast_seal_discrepancy.csv.

What this page does not do

  • It does not adopt the tranche. v2 is published beside v1; the model still fits on the frozen 137 games, because adopting it re-fits every rating and changes published lines. That is the owner's call (P09).
  • It does not recompute the backtest. The identity table states a structural fact — the 13 added games are all in Week 10, so no evaluation week's training set moves — and checks it row by row.
  • It does not fill a single market price. No licensed odds feed exists, so the effect is measured against our own v1 line and the naive reference line, never against a market (F07, F32).
  • It does not claim season-wide venues or a Week 11 injury report. Both are published as coverage numbers with dates and flagged (F111, F112).
