The dated corpus tranche: v1 frozen, v2 published beside it

Why this page exists. The pre-registered backtest corpus is data/games_2026_results.csv — 137 games (weeks 1–9 = the brief's 136-game log, plus the Week 10 Thursday game). Thirteen Week 10 games played on 15 and 16 November 2026 are settled and were transcribed from the official by-week page, but editing a pre-registered corpus in place would silently change every published metric. So the 13 games are published as a parallel corpus (data/corpus_v2_2026.csv, 150 games) and v1 is left byte-frozen. This is proposal P09 / readiness D29 and D64 finally carrying a number instead of a request.

Flagged: the seal and the published line disagree on one game (F110)

This was found while writing the check above, and it is an irregularity, not a rounding difference. The pre-kickoff forecast log is sealed with SHA-256 so a later edit is detectable. Recomputing that digest from the v1 fit reproduces the published seal (5aaf29db…) only when the neutral-site flag is ignored; applying the flag — which is what the published Week 11 line does — produces a different digest. The one fixture affected is the Mexico City game, MIN at SF: the sealed log froze 2.5 / 38.5 and the published line reads 2.9 / 38.9. All twelve other fixtures agree to the decimal, because no other Week 11 game is neutral. The cause is visible in scripts/model.py: the log is sealed with predict(main, home, away) while the same game is originated into model_lines_week11.csv with the neutral flag applied.

Nothing was overwritten. The published line is the correct treatment under F71 (a neutral site gets no home-field term), so reverting it would introduce an error; what is stale is the seal. Two ways to close, both owner decisions: (a) re-seal from the neutral-aware prediction and record the old digest as superseded, or (b) keep the v1 seal as the historical record and add a v2 seal beside it. Until one is chosen, the leaderboard stays unranked. Reproduce with python3 scripts/corpus_v2_effect.py; register data/forecast_seal_discrepancy.csv.

What this page does not do

  • It does not adopt the tranche. v2 is published beside v1; the model still fits on the frozen 137 games, because adopting it re-fits every rating and changes published lines. That is the owner's call (P09).
  • It does not recompute the backtest. The identity table states a structural fact — the 13 added games are all in Week 10, so no evaluation week's training set moves — and checks it row by row.
  • It does not fill a single market price. No licensed odds feed exists, so the effect is measured against our own v1 line and the naive reference line, never against a market (F07, F32).
  • It does not claim season-wide venues or a Week 11 injury report. Both are published as coverage numbers with dates and flagged (F111, F112).

Both total estimators, every week, with the gap (T112)

The brief asks for every model total to be published twice until one wins. The two honest competitors for the total in this repository are the ratings total (T1: the same ridge fit that originates the side, summed over both teams) and the league-average total (T2: 2 x league points per team per game on games strictly before the week — the published naive benchmark). Both are shown for all 89 walk-forward games, with the gap, the realised total and which was closer. Neither is a market number: with no licensed odds feed (F07/F32) "winning" can only mean closer to what happened.

The collapse rule is written down before the result is read, and it did not fire. Pooled mean absolute error: T1 11.077 points, T2 10.964. The paired difference (T2 error − T1 error, positive favours T1) is -0.113 with a game-cluster bootstrap interval of [-0.982, 0.805] and a sign-flip p of 0.7986; T1 was closer on 43 games, T2 on 46, ties 0. The interval spans zero, so the decision is no winner — interval spans zero and both estimators stay published. The mean absolute gap between them is 3.516 points — which is the number that makes the choice matter: on a typical game the two methods disagree by more than a field goal's worth of total, and neither has earned the right to be the only one shown.

A property of the published model that this comparison made visible: the fit gives the home side +h and does not subtract h from the away side, so the fitted home-field term enters the total as well as the margin (it is 0.62 points in Week 4 and negative by Week 10; the column is published per game). The comparison reproduces the published model rather than quietly correcting it: the build refuses to publish unless both estimators match data/model_backtest.csv at one decimal on all 89 rows, which is how this was noticed in the first place. Making the total venue-neutral is a model change, not a display change, and it is flagged for the model owner rather than applied here (F113).

The weekly "what changed" diff, published instead of a static snapshot (T114)

Three columns, and the honest version shows why two of them are nearly empty. The brief asks for a diff of rank change, rating change and availability change, stored per week rather than overwritten. All three are here, one row per club: the rating change is the same ridge fit re-run before the Week 10 Sunday slate and after it (net rating = attack − defence, i.e. expected margin against an average opponent), the rank change is the movement of that ordering, and the availability change is counted from the dated injury delta. Nothing is a level; each is a difference between two dated reads.

The finding. On the frozen 137-game feed, one game separates the two snapshots — the Week 10 Thursday result — so 32 of 32 clubs do not move a single rank place. That is not a bug in the diff, it is the corpus: the other 13 Week 10 games are outside the frozen feed (F96). The same two snapshots re-run on the parallel 150-game corpus move things materially — SF +2.52 pts (10→5), DAL +2.22 pts (3→1), NE -2.09 pts (14→22), DET -2.01 pts (7→12), JAX +1.63 pts (20→14), TEN +1.49 pts (22→16). Both columns are published side by side; neither is presented as the rating.

Availability is published as a re-read delta, not as week-over-week news. 3 clubs moved inside the same Week 10 report between the two reads (IND: 2 rows, BAL: 6 rows, LAC: 26 rows), and the rows are labelled that way. A true week-over-week availability change cannot be computed because no Week 11 report has been published (F111) — so the column stays a re-read delta with its note, never a number dressed up as a weekly change.
