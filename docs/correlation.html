Correlation — the plan, with every value blank

No correlation value is published here, and none is guessed. A correlation number without paired, settled, point-in-time data would be fabricated. What is published is the exact measurement each pair needs, so the work is ready the moment the data is licensed. The one thing this page insists on: a parlay is not the product of its legs. If the joint rate differs from the product of the marginals, the ticket is mispriced by construction.

Why the naive number is wrong

Multiply two independent probabilities and you get the joint probability only if the legs are independent. In the same game they are not: a quarterback's passing yards and his receiver's yards share the same throws; a running back's rushes and his receptions trade off against game script; a team total and a spread can be settled by the same scoring play. The correct measure is the observed joint rate minus the product of the marginals, with an interval, on paired settled legs. See the NIST glossary for the underlying definitions.
