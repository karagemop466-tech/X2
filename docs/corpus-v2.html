The pre-registered backtest corpus, published twice

The brief asks for the game log to become a pre-registered backtest corpus, and for every model total to be published twice until one wins. Both are done here as one decision: the corpus that has been published since the first session (89 games, weeks 4–10) and a second corpus that adds the 13 settled Week 10 games transcribed from the official by-week page (102 games pooled). The two are named separately, the 89 published rows are reproduced before anything is written, and a reader can see which rows are old and which are new in the provenance column.

The two corpora disagree, and that is the result. On the published 89 games the model beats its own naive reference line by 1.736 points of mean absolute error per game. On the 13 games it had never been scored against it is 0.591 points behind the same reference line. Pooled, the gain falls to 1.439 points. The same comparison on totals is worse than that in every cut: the model does not beat the reference total even on the published corpus (-0.113 points pooled, -0.266 on all 102). Publishing only the first number would be the exact selection the brief forbids, so the holdout number is published beside it and the leadership question stays open (Flag F110).

What each row stores

Every row is a probability plus the inputs that produced it, not a pick: the model margin, the model total, the reference line the probability is measured against, the residual scale and where that scale came from, the three probabilities, the realised outcome, both absolute errors, the fit size and the provenance of the game row. That is the storage requirement, applied to the corpus itself.

Why the published feed was not spliced

Re-fitting on 150 games instead of 137 changes the Week 11 lines. Those lines are already frozen and SHA-256 sealed in data/prospective_forecast_log.csv with a frozen_before_kickoff flag, which is the only thing that makes them pre-kickoff evidence. Re-sealing them after the Week 10 results were known would convert a forecast into a retrodiction and destroy the log's probative value. So the 13 games enter as a second corpus with its own name and the sealed log is untouched. The published Week 11 numbers therefore still come from the 137-game fit, and that is stated wherever they appear. The alternative — splice, re-fit, re-originate, re-seal — remains available to the owner as a deliberate choice, recorded on the Review console; it is not a data-capture side effect.

Reproducing this page

python3 scripts/corpus_v2.py rebuilds both registers. It fails the run if any of the 89 published rows differs from the published value by more than 0.055 points (the published one-decimal rounding), if the backfill register does not hold exactly 13 games, or if the pooled corpus does not hold exactly 102. It imports the verified model construction from scripts/forecast_records.py rather than re-typing it, so the two cannot drift apart. The residual scale for Week 10 is estimated only on games before Week 10; for Week 4, where no earlier scored week exists, the in-sample residual of the training fit is used and the row says so in residual_sd_source.

Reconciled with the parallel results register

On the same day, session 82b83a64 built Results register v2 (data/results_feed_v2_2026.csv) over the same 13 settled games plus the eight completed international games, and re-ran scripts/model.py on four corpora in a sandbox. Its unflagged 150-game fit reproduces this page's Week 10 holdout count exactly — the model closer on 5 of 13 games — while its flagged fit gives 3 of 13: the only difference is the neutral-site treatment, which re-estimates the ratings and moves rows across the line. The registers therefore do not disagree about the model, and both say the model is behind its reference on the slice it had never been scored against (F71, F125).
