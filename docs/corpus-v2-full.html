1. What changed between the versions

Two inputs differ, and only two. Thirteen settled Week 10 games were added — transcribed from the official by-week page, never computed from records — and eight settled games were re-flagged as neutral sites, because data/international_games_2026.csv lists them on the official 2026 international slate and the v1 corpus left every neutral_site cell blank. The code, the seed, the ridge penalty, the bet threshold and the metric definitions are identical: scripts/model_v2.py executes scripts/model.py unmodified.

v1 was not touched. Before the v2 run, all 15 files scripts/model.py writes were hashed; after it, all 15 hashes were identical. The build fails if any of them changed. That is the whole guarantee: the published numbers could not move.

The 13 rows are correct, and the old reconciliation was stale. All 32 clubs were read from the official standings on 2026-11-18 and compared, club by club, on W-L-T, points for and points against. With the 13 rows added, 32 of 32 clubs match. The published 137-game feed matches 6 of 32. The old "all 32 teams reconcile" check compared the feed with a standings read taken before the Week 10 Sunday games, so both sides were stale in the same direction (F106). The per-club table below shows both versions against today's standings; a "NO" in the v1 rows is the defect, not a transcription error in the added rows.

2. Every metric that moves

A reader comparing a number on one page with a number on another needs to know which version each one came from. This is the register that makes that possible: 106 metrics, v1 beside v2, with the signed change. Every other page on this site shows v1.

The model's edge over the naive line is smaller on the complete log

1.38 points on v2 against 1.74 points on v1. That is the single most important number on this page and it moves in the direction that is less flattering: the model's advantage over a naive season-differential benchmark shrinks by -0.3600 points when the 13 missing games are added. The naive line also improves (10.62 against 10.89), so part of the apparent v1 edge was a small-sample artefact of the week that was missing. The calibration slope moves towards 1.0 and its interval still contains 1.0, so neither version demonstrates calibration.

3. What this does and does not license

  • It does not rank anything. The competition leaderboard is prospective-only and remains unranked in both versions; these are research backtest scores (F42, F87). Adding 13 games does not create a pre-registration.
  • It does not produce a return. Every "bet" is against a naive reference line, not a market price, because no licensed odds feed exists (F07, F51). ROI is still not computable on either version.
  • It does not settle P09. Only the owner can retire v1. If option B is later accepted as the published version, every page carrying a v1 number needs the swap; this page exists so that swap is a decision about one version tag, not a quiet rewrite.
  • The ninth international game is not in the corpus. The official slate has nine games; MIN at SF (Estadio Banorte, Mexico City) is Week 11 and unplayed, so a settled corpus can carry at most eight. That is the number in neutral_games_flagged, not nine.

5. A second pipeline, built independently, reaches the same answer

While this page was being written, a separate session (f04dda73) built its own corpus from the same 13 games by a different route — scripts/corpus_v2.py, 102 games, published on the Pre-registered corpus page. The two pipelines share no code, and they converge:

  • Both score the same 102 out-of-sample games and both reproduce the published 1.74-point gain on the 89-game control before adding anything.
  • Both land in the same place once the 13 games are added: this page reports 1.38 points of margin gain, the other pipeline reports 1.439 — a 0.06-point spread between two independent implementations, both far below the 1.74 on the published corpus.
  • They differ on one modelling choice, and it is not settled here. This build flags the eight settled international games as neutral sites; the other does not. A 0.3-point difference is compatible with that, but neither build can establish which treatment is correct without a pre-registered rule. It is put to the owner as a question rather than quietly resolved.
  • The sharper test is the other page's, not this one's. It cuts the 13 new games out as a holdout and finds the model behind its own naive line on them (−0.591 points). This page's walk-forward scores all 102 games together, which dilutes exactly that effect. A reader should take the holdout result as the more conservative reading of the same evidence.
