What the values say

  • The model's margin forecast is genuinely associated with the actual margin (r = 0.321, p = 0.002), and more so than the naive reference line (r = 0.274, p = 0.009). That association is a historical backtest diagnostic, not a leaderboard score; the prospective leaderboard is currently unranked.
  • Game totals are close to unpredictable with the inputs used here: predicted totals correlate r = 0.092 (p = 0.39) with actual totals, and the totals strategy shows no accuracy gain at all.
  • Within a game, the two teams' scores do not co-move (r = 0.042) and blowouts are not associated with higher totals (r = 0.021). Both matter for parlay pricing, and both are essentially zero in this sample.
  • Last week's margin barely predicts this week's (r = 0.087, n = 228 team-game pairs that are not independent; published as descriptive with no interval or p-value, Flag F55 resolved), which is why recency weighting did not help and why short hot streaks should not be trusted.
  • The calibration slope is 0.885 with a 95% interval of 0.328 to 1.443 (p = 0.68 against slope 1.0, corrected 2026-11-14): the interval is wide and includes both over-shy and over-confident readings — the model's scale is not yet pinned down (C09, model diagnostic).
  • The parlay leg correlation is negative but uncertain (phi = -0.299 on 24 tickets, p = 0.16). It is a warning not to price parlays as if legs were independent, not a usable estimate.
