Replacement depth

The brief asks not "who is out" but how deep the fall is when the starter is out. This is the slot-led detail register: for each affected position it records how many charted bodies stand between the absent player and the next one, and how much of that depth the same unit of absence has already consumed. It is deliberately a separate file from the availability-led register, joined by game number, so a correction in one cannot silently rewrite the other.

1. What counts as verified here

Both club chart pages read for this register label themselves unofficial and carry no date — "Unofficial Depth Chart Compiled by Lions Football Communications", and a chart "published by the writers of Patriots.com". They are the clubs' own publications, which is the closest thing to an official depth source that exists (Flags F45, F18), and the read date is recorded on every row because a chart is an alignment snapshot that decays. The availability label comes from the league's own report; the depth position comes from the club chart; the two are not the same kind of statement and are not merged into one column.

2. The register

Depth consumed names the other absences at the same slot in the same game, so a reader can see whether a backup is the first next man up or the third. Shared backup flags a single body covering two vacancies. Why blank is filled whenever a field is empty on purpose.

3. Three things this register is built to show

  • A replacement can be the last body in the unit. At Detroit's centre slot two bodies are charted and one is Out, so the "first replacement available" is the final name on the page (RD-02). At left guard in Foxborough the ratio is two of two, and the single remaining body is itself listed questionable and charted at both guard slots (RD-06) — one player covering two vacancies.
  • A returner standing in for a receiver is not the same event as a third receiver rising to first. Both are one line of chart text and completely different exposures (RD-01 against the returner entry at RD-05).
  • Absences do not arrive one at a time. The depth consumed in the unit field is what stops a count of absent players being read as a count of depth problems — the failure mode the brief named (information gap G13). RD-11 is the mirror case: an absence two lines down the chart is recorded as such so it is not counted like a starter problem.

Where a player on the league's availability report appears on no row at all of his club's chart, the depth fields are left blank with that stated as the reason, rather than filled from a third-party chart the official-source rule excludes. A chart omission means "this source cannot place him", not "he is fine" (F62, suggestion S33). Rows RD-13 and RD-14 are blank for a different reason again — no chart captured for that club this week, and a player on the chart who is not on the league report — which is recorded rather than guessed.
