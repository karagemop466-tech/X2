Key findings

  • Verification. This session re-read the official Week 10 report in full and compared it row by row with the stored data: all 345 rows across all 28 teams matched, and the 14 game headers matched. The spot-check request in Flag F10 is closed for one reviewer pass; see the Verification record.
  • Coverage. The Week 10 NFL.com report lists 345 players across 28 teams in 14 games. The official NFL schedule identifies CHI, DEN, PHI, TB as on bye (Flag F05); this is confirmed from the schedule, not inferred from the injury report.
  • Game status. 66 players are Out, 10 Doubtful and 84 Questionable. 185 listed players carry no game-status label on the report.
  • Where the exposure is. Out or Doubtful players by side of ball: offense 42, defense 32, special teams 2. Most-exposed teams by Out plus Doubtful count: DAL Cowboys (5 Out, 1 Doubtful), IND Colts (6 Out, 0 Doubtful), GB Packers (4 Out, 1 Doubtful), HOU Texans (5 Out, 0 Doubtful), MIN Vikings (5 Out, 0 Doubtful).
  • Quarterbacks. The news headlines on the same page report Lamar Jackson (Ravens) and Malik Willis (Dolphins) on injured reserve, and Sam Darnold (Seahawks) ruled out for Week 10. Their replacements are not verified in depth-chart data (see Flag F06).
  • Strategy research. Seven pre-registered strategies have now been graded walk-forward on the official 2026 results; four qualify and three reach the top tier. They are graded on forecast accuracy, not on price: no odds feed is connected, so nothing here is a market result and no reward is paid (Flags F32, F33).
  • Originated lines (new). A ridge attack/defence model fitted on the 137 official 2026 results now produces our own spreads and totals for all 13 Week 11 games. Out of sample it is 1.74 points closer to the actual margin per game than a naive published reference line (mean absolute error 9.16 vs 10.89 points over 89 games, 95% bootstrap interval 0.76 to 2.74 points). The calibration slope is 0.885 (SE 0.28), so the lines are neither over-shy nor over-confident.
  • Leaderboard (new). 7 strategies were pre-registered and graded walk-forward: 4 qualify and 3 reach the top tier. The totals strategy shows no skill and the parlay strategy fails on sample size. Every row shows how many bets it would need for a decisive test, and none has enough (Flag F33).
  • What the leaderboard does not prove. Bets are graded against a naive reference line, because this project has no licensed odds feed. Beating a noisy benchmark is easier than beating a sportsbook price, so no hit rate here may be read as market return (Flag F32).
  • Results verified line by line. Every one of the 137 transcribed games was reconciled against the official standings: all 32 teams match exactly on wins, losses, ties, points for and points against.
  • Data rights. NFL.com terms prohibit systematic retrieval to compile a database absent express prior written consent. No permission is recorded here for the published injury snapshot; do not expand or automate this use pending review (Flag F16).
  • Legal. Government of Japan guidance says online sports betting accessed from Japan is gambling and prohibited there; this is Japan-specific and does not decide contest or reward mechanics. No legal opinion is provided (Flag F09).

How a decision should be made from this

  1. Use the originated lines as a forecast, not as a price. A forecast says what the model expects; a price says what it costs. Only the first exists here.
  2. Judge strategies on the skill column (points of forecast gain), not on the hit-rate column, which is measured against a naive benchmark.
  3. Check the required sample size before acting: every strategy on the board is currently short of a decisive test.
  4. Resolve the gates in Flagged for review (F16 data rights, F19 jurisdiction, F31 player data) before adding data, automation or rewards.
