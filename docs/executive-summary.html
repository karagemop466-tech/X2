  • Game status. 70 players are Out, 11 Doubtful and 90 Questionable. 176 listed players carry no game-status label on the report.
  • Where the exposure is. Out or Doubtful players by side of ball: offense 44, defense 35, special teams 2. Most-exposed teams by Out plus Doubtful count: DAL Cowboys (5 Out, 1 Doubtful), IND Colts (6 Out, 0 Doubtful), GB Packers (4 Out, 1 Doubtful), HOU Texans (5 Out, 0 Doubtful), MIN Vikings (5 Out, 0 Doubtful).
  • Quarterbacks. The news headlines on the same page report Lamar Jackson (Ravens) and Malik Willis (Dolphins) on injured reserve, and Sam Darnold (Seahawks) ruled out for Week 10. Official news names Drew Lock for Darnold (2nd string on the club chart) and Kyle McCord for Willis ("in line for first start"); no replacement is named for Lamar Jackson and the Ravens chart omits him entirely. Every row and chart rank is on the Replacement depth page (Flags F06/F45).
  • Strategy research. Historical walk-forward analyses are retained only as backtest research, not competition rankings. The prospective leaderboard has seven registered entries and zero graded prospective samples, so it remains unranked and no reward is paid. No odds feed is connected.
  • Originated lines (new). A ridge attack/defence model fitted on the 137 official 2026 results now produces our own spreads and totals for all 13 Week 11 games. Out of sample it is 1.74 points closer to the actual margin per game than a naive published reference line (mean absolute error 9.16 vs 10.89 points over 89 games, 95% bootstrap interval 0.76 to 2.74 points). The calibration slope is 0.885 (SE 0.28; 95% interval 0.328 to 1.443, p = 0.683 against slope 1.0), so there is no evidence the lines are over-shy or over-confident (Flag F48 corrected 2026-11-14).
  • Origination order and fair/market/difference (new). Each market is originated in a fixed order — side first, then total, then moneyline — and the model's fair line, the market line and the difference are published as separate columns on the Model & Week 11 lines page. The market columns are blank: no licensed odds feed exists, so no market price can be confirmed from an official source (Flags F07, F31).
  • Price-snapshot store (new). A store now records the opening and closing number for every market this project originates — 306 rows covering the Week 11 slate and the out-of-sample window, side, total and moneyline. No official source publishes opening or closing NFL market numbers, so every opening and closing value is blank and flagged rather than inferred (Flags F07, F31, F51). The schema is in place for a rights-cleared odds feed to fill it.
  • Signal separated from staking. The prospective leaderboard has no ranking yet; when it becomes eligible, signal scores and staking returns will remain separate. ROI is blank because no licensed prices exist (Flags F07, F32, F51).
  • Demotion gate (new). A demotion gate (G9) is defined and armed: a live strategy is sent back to paper the moment any of G1–G8 fails, the skill interval includes zero, or the mean edge turns negative. No strategy is live today — the qualification is disputed by the independent cross-check (F42) and wagering and rewards stay disabled — so every strategy is Paper (Flags F17, F42).
  • Leaderboard. Seven candidate strategy definitions exist, but none has a complete immutable pre-registration or a graded prospective window. The competition page is therefore unranked; retrospective backtest numbers are not displayed there.
  • Backtest boundary. Historic model-vs-reference comparisons are available on the Backtest page only. No historical score is used as a leaderboard entry or reward qualification.
  • Signal and staking are separated. The future prospective board will rank only pre-registered signal metrics and show staking separately; today it has no graded prospective sample and no rank. See the Market tables page.
  • Price-snapshot store (new). All 39 originated Week 11 markets — side first, then total, then moneyline — are tracked in a store with opening and closing fields. Every price cell is blank and marked unverified: no authorised, timestamped price source exists (Flags F07, F43). The store is the piece that makes closing-line value and ROI computable the moment real prices land; until then, no ROI is estimated.
  • Demotion gate defined (new). Seven rules (D1-D7, gate G9) define how a live strategy returns to paper: signal-interval collapse, multiplicity failure, sample loss, source revision, lost clearance, and a CLV test once prices exist. Staking results can neither promote nor rescue a strategy. The rules are proposals — thresholds await the owner's statistical plan (Flag F17) — and no strategy is live, so the gate is inert today.
  • Depth of the replacement (new). Replacement events are now tracked with an explicit string depth, not just the fact of a replacement — across two tables: RD01–RD07 (Detroit and New England, where club-published charts verify order: the Patriots' backfield is already down to its 3rd/4th string, the Lions' cornerback room to its 2nd/3rd) and the T42 availability table (session 8a0bf37e), which adds fetched club-chart checks for all eleven availability rows — SEA, MIA, BAL, CLE, AZ and NYG with ranks where an official statement names the replacement, plus GB, LAR, KC and IND (session d2915d86, Flag F53 resolved) where the chart order is recorded but the rank stays blank because no official source names a replacement. Where no chart confirms the order the depth cell is blank and flagged (F06).
  • Results verified line by line. Every one of the 137 transcribed games was reconciled against the official standings: all 32 teams match exactly on wins, losses, ties, points for and points against.
  • Data rights. NFL.com terms prohibit systematic retrieval to compile a database absent express prior written consent. No permission is recorded here for the published injury snapshot; do not expand or automate this use pending review (Flag F16).
  • Legal. Government of Japan guidance says online sports betting accessed from Japan is gambling and prohibited there; this is Japan-specific and does not decide contest or reward mechanics. No legal opinion is provided (Flag F09).

How a decision should be made from this

  1. Use the originated lines as a forecast, not as a price. A forecast says what the model expects; a price says what it costs. Only the first exists here.
  2. Do not interpret the historical skill or hit-rate statistics as competition standings. They belong to the Backtest research page only; the prospective Leaderboard remains unranked until grading is complete.
  3. Check the required sample size before acting: every strategy on the board is currently short of a decisive test.
  4. Resolve the gates in Flagged for review (F16 data rights, F19 jurisdiction, F31 player data) before adding data, automation or rewards.
