The five things that matter

  1. The data you have is solid; the data you need is not here. The Week 10 injury snapshot re-verified cleanly: 345 rows across 28 teams and 14 games, 345 of 345 rows matching the official page on position, injury, practice status and game status (1380 field comparisons). Verification
  2. Starter identity turned out to be officially verifiable after all. The NFL publishes no pregame depth chart, so a "1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th string" rank cannot be sourced. But the official NFL gamebook publishes the starting lineup by slot, the substitutions and the inactive list. Nineteen starter-identity rows are verified, and the earlier flag saying no official starter data existed is corrected as F31. Starters and depth
  3. You cannot originate a line yet, and the reason is data, not method. Twenty signal definitions, a seventeen-gate skill engine, ten anti-luck tests and a full correlation plan are written and published. Not one of them has a value, because there is no licensed price series, no historical results ledger and no paired settled outcomes. Signals · Skill engine
  4. "Remove luck" is the wrong target; bounding it is the right one. Football results carry large irreducible variance. What can be enforced is that a lucky result cannot pass: bootstrap intervals, a published search log, multiplicity-adjusted decisions and a held-out block. Anti-luck
  5. Two gates block almost everything. Data rights (DQ-01) and jurisdiction plus contest design (DQ-02). NFL terms prohibit systematic retrieval to compile a database without written consent, and official Japanese guidance treats online sports betting as a criminal offence. Until those two are answered, expanding collection or enabling a reward would be imprudent. Decision gates

The three decisions that unlock the most work

  1. DQ-01 — data rights. Settle permission or a licence. Until then the site stays a source-linked summary and collection is not expanded or automated (F16).
  2. DQ-02 — jurisdiction and contest design. Obtain a written legal opinion covering rewards and advertising before any prize is described (F09, EG16).
  3. DQ-09 — historical depth. Decide how many seasons of schedules and gamebooks to collect; one week cannot support a single statistical gate.

What would change the picture fastest

  1. A written data-rights answer. This single reply determines whether the existing snapshot can stay published and whether any collection can expand (D01).
  2. A licensed odds feed. Without prices, no independent line can be compared, graded or scored for closing-line value (D02).
  3. Rights-cleared history. Without a settled-outcome archive there is no sample, so no strategy can be validated or ranked (D03).
  4. An official depth source for the remaining games. Only one Week 10 gamebook exists so far; the other thirteen games had not been played at snapshot time (V009 on the Verification page).
