Strategy leaderboard

How to read this. Signal and staking are separated on purpose. The signal block ranks strategies on forecast skill: how many points per game closer the strategy's forecast landed to the actual result than the naive reference line, across all 89 out-of-sample games. It is a forecast-accuracy measure, not profit. The staking block records the staking plan and its return — which is blank, because no licensed odds feed exists, so no staking return can be computed (Flags F07, F32, F46). A lucky staking run therefore cannot mask a weak signal: the two are read separately. The hit rate and edge columns describe a paper record against the naive reference line and must not be read as a market result (Flag F32). Required sample size is how many bets the strategy would need, at the effect size it observed, for an 80%-power test; every strategy on this board is currently short.

Skill versus luck — the columns that answer a different question

A rank answers "who finished first". It does not answer "who is better". The columns below separate the two. Weeks positive re-measures each strategy's skill inside every week of the window: a rule that only looks good because of one hot stretch shows up immediately. Week sign-test p asks whether that per-week split is better than a coin flip. A row that is positive but not repeatable across weeks is a row that has not yet earned a rank.

What this table actually shows

  • The best rule is positive but not yet repeatable. M1 gains 1.74 points per game on average, its 95% bootstrap interval excludes zero, and both halves of the window agree — yet its per-week skill is positive in only 5 of 7 weeks, which a coin flip produces about 0.4531 of the time (sign test, p = 0.4531). That is the honest state of the evidence: promising, unproven.
  • Luck share is not a measure of any single bet. It is the fraction of the interval that lies on the wrong side of zero, i.e. how much of the observed record a chance process could reproduce.
  • Nothing here rescues a strategy that fails the gates. The luck columns are an additional filter, not a substitute for the nine validation gates (G1-G9).

Demotion gate — live back to paper

The demotion gate (G9) is armed. A strategy that is live is demoted back to paper the moment any of G1–G8 fails on the latest window, or its skill 95% interval includes zero, or its mean edge turns negative. No strategy is live today: the qualification is disputed by the independent cross-check (Flag F42), the gate cut-offs await owner approval (F17) and wagering and rewards stay disabled (F09, F16, F19). Every strategy is therefore Paper; the demotion-safe column shows which ones would keep (or earn) live status once the gates are approved and the disagreement resolved.

Reward structure — proposed, not approved, nothing paid

  • Tier 1 (gold) — passes all eight qualification gates. Proposed: top leaderboard recognition.
  • Tier 2 (silver) — passes G1, G2, G3, G4, G5 and G6. Proposed: mid-tier recognition.
  • Tier 3 (bronze) — passes G1, G2, G3 and G5. Proposed: tracking only, no promotion.
  • Not qualified — fails any of G1, G2, G3 or G5. No tier, no promotion.

The competition is between strategies inside this repository, not between people, and no reward has monetary value. Any reward, entry fee, referral or wager mechanic stays disabled pending jurisdiction confirmation and qualified legal review (Flags F09, F19) and data-rights clearance (F16). Approve, amend or reject these tiers on the Suggestions page; nothing here has been implemented on the basis of a suggestion.
