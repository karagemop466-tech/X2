Player props and prop combinations

The brief asks for player prop bet combos. This page separates two questions that are usually conflated: are prop outcomes available from an official source? and are prop prices available? The answers differ, and that difference decides what can be built.

Outcomes: available officially

  • NFL.com publishes per-player, per-game logs (passing, rushing, receiving, scoring) on each player's /stats/logs/ page. That is an official source for the outcome of any prop.
  • This repository holds no player-level game series (F31), so no prop has been graded.

Prices: not available

  • A prop bet is a contract between a bettor and a book. The league does not publish prop lines.
  • Without a licensed price feed there is no prop market line, no prop edge and no prop ROI (F07).
  • So a prop can be originated and graded but would still not be priced.

Prop combinations — the dependency problem

A prop combo is two or more props on the same game. Its price depends on the correlation between the legs, and that correlation is exactly what nobody has measured in this repository. Positive correlation makes the true joint probability higher than the product of the legs, so the naive price is too long; negative correlation makes it too short. Until the correlation exists, a combo price would be a guess dressed as arithmetic.

What a reader should conclude

  • The side/total phi of -0.30 on 24 tickets is the only measured same-game dependence in this repository, and 24 tickets is far too few to price anything (F33).
  • The model's own side and total errors correlate at a small negative value, which means the independence shortcut is at least not obviously wrong for this model — not that it is right.
  • Prop-vs-prop correlation cannot be measured until a player game series is transcribed (P02).
