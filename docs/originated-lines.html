Originated lines — side, then total, then moneyline

These are our own numbers, not market numbers. The three markets are originated in a fixed order because each step consumes the previous one: the attack/defence ratings give a home margin (the side); the same ratings give expected points for each team, and their sum is the total; the margin plus the walk-forward residual spread gives a win probability, and that probability gives a moneyline price. Every market column is blank because no licensed odds feed is connected (F07). Each row links to the official NFL.com game page, which confirms the matchup and kickoff — not any price. scripts/originate.py re-fits the ratings independently and refuses to publish if any spread or total disagrees with scripts/model.py by more than 0.05 points; the two agree exactly today (largest difference 0.000).

Step 1 — the side, step 2 — the total, step 3 — the moneyline

The side is the home team's predicted margin: positive means the home team is favoured by that many points. The reference column is the naive benchmark the model has to beat (season point differential per game plus a 2.5-point home field, and no home-field term at a neutral site). The edge is the model minus the reference. The fair price columns are zero-vig prices implied by the model, not prices anyone has offered. The market and difference columns stay blank until a market line exists.

Our own opening and current number for every market

The store holds an opening value (fitted on the 136 games of weeks 1-9, the number the model would have published before the Week 10 Thursday game) and a current value (fitted on all 137 games, the number published now). The move between the two is how much one extra game moved our own number: the largest side move is 0.4 points and the mean is 0.06, so the ratings are stable. The closing value is reserved: by definition it is the last number published before kickoff, so it is recorded only once the games have been played. See price snapshots for the store this extends.

Two caveats found while building this page

  • Home-team bias (F76). Graded against the number the model itself published, the home side covered 36, lost 52 and pushed 1 over 89 out-of-sample games; the mean signed error is -0.83 points (SE 1.30, t = -0.64). That is not statistically distinguishable from zero, so the line is published as computed, but it is the direction to watch.
  • Neutral site — now handled. One of the thirteen Week 11 games (MIN at SF, Mexico City) carries neutral_site = Y on the official schedule, and it is the one row on the table above that is published with the home-field term dropped. The remaining half of that defect is still open and is not solved here: all 137 completed historical rows carry a blank neutral_site value, so the historical fit still treats any neutral-site game among them as a home game (F71).
