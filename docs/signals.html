How a signal becomes a line

  1. Define it before you look. The definition, the cutoff and the metric are written down first (gate EG1).
  2. Prove it is knowable at the cutoff. Every input must have been visible before the bet (gate EG2).
  3. Beat a declared baseline. A league mean and the availability signal alone are the two baselines (EG3).
  4. Check calibration, not just accuracy. A probability that says 60 percent should happen about 60 percent of the time (EG4).
  5. Survive the anti-luck suite. Bootstrap interval, multiplicity control and a held-out block (see Anti-luck).
  6. Only then price it. The price is the market's number; your number is the forecast. The gap is the only thing worth betting.

Single bet, parlays and player-prop combos

Three families are separated because they fail differently. A single bet fails when the forecast is wrong. A same-game combo fails when the joint probability is wrong even though every single leg is right, which is a correlation problem. A cross-game parlay fails when costs and settlement are ignored. The Correlation plan page holds the dependence plan; nothing has been measured.
