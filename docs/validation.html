How the walk-forward test works

  1. Start at the earliest week with any history. Ratings are fitted on strictly earlier weeks only.
  2. Project every game in the current week, then move to the next week and refit. Nothing from a later week ever enters an earlier projection.
  3. A game is scored only if every rule could produce a projection for it, so all rules are compared on an identical set.
  4. Probability scores use a residual scale estimated from earlier weeks only, never from the week being scored.
  5. Intervals come from resampling the games, not from a formula that assumes a distribution.

What validation cannot do

Out-of-sample testing removes hindsight and quantifies uncertainty. It cannot remove variance, and it cannot guarantee a result in a sport decided by a handful of plays. A rule that passes every gate on 96 games can still lose over the next 96.
